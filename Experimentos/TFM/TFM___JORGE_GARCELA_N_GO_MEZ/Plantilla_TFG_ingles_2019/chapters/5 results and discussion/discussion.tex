In Table~\ref{table:classification_tasks_performance} we present a comparison of the performance of the three different approaches followed. As discussed in Section~\ref{implementation_dl}, the DL approach was limited by the high computational cost, which prevented us from testing a broad range of hyperparameters. Consequently, the time spent in these experiments was 2.978 times less than in the ML approach. The reason was that although the total time for ML experiments was higher, we performed more different experiments, in particular 18.956 times more. As a result, the total average time per experiment for DL was 6.369 times higher, which explains the fewer number of experiments. Moreover, while the number of experiments for each DL task in Table~\ref{table:classification_tasks_performance} was 15, the actual number of successful experiments varied slightly: 11 for"Análisis General", 14 for "Contenido Negativo" and 14 for "Insultos", as explained in Section~\ref{implementation_dl}.

Another interesting highlight was the performance of the zero-shot capabilities in the Generative AI approach, especially for the "Contenido Negativo" task, where the mean time per experiment was just 0.043 hours and 0.026 hours for "Insultos". Remarkably, in 4.186 times less time, we were able to conduct 4.267 times more experiments than with the DL approach. However, when compared to the ML approach, we performed 4.443 times more experiments in ML, investing 12.467 times more in total time, yet achieving a better Mean Time per Experiment of 0.214 hours, compared to 0.254 for the GenAI approach.

Overall, the ML approach consistently delivered the best results across all tasks, while DL and GenAI approaches, though promising, still required significant improvements. Additionally, it is worth noting that the GenAI models were the quickest, primarily due to the zero-shot learning approach. However, this came at the cost of accuracy, as the lack of training made it hard for these general models to handle complex classifications, such as "Contenido Negativo" and "Insultos".

In the "Análisis General" task, as seen in Table~\ref{table:AG_performance}, the ML model ({\agmlpteup}) demonstrated the highest overall performance, achieving a weighted average F1-Score of 0.946. It performed exceptionally well in both the "Comentario Positivo" (F1 of 0.966) and "Comentario Negativo" (F1 of 0.878) categories and completed the task in a relatively efficient time of 1166.859 seconds. The DL model ({\agfcbeto}) also delivered strong results, particularly in "Comentario Positivo" (F1 of 0.944), but required significantly more processing time (12331.273 seconds) and showed weaker performance in handling "Comentario Negativo" (F1 of 0.815). The GenAI model ({\agmistralsevenb}), while the fastest, completing the task in just 708.685 seconds, decreased in accuracy, especially in the "Comentario Negativo" category, where it only achieved an F1 score of 0.690, leading to a lower weighted average F1 score of 0.802.
