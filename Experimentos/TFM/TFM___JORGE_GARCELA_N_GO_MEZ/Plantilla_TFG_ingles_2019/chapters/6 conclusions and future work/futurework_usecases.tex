One interesting direction to address the complexities mentioned above is exploring methods that "do not rely on the assumption that a single (gold) interpretation exists for each category", such as multi-label approaches and learning with disagreement \cite{uma-2022}. This direction could help our models better handle the subjective content present in HS, where annotators may disagree on labels or where content might fit into multiple categories at the same time, like in "Contenido Negativo" and "Insultos".

Other areas for future exploration are interpretability and explainability, which remain key challenges in NLP applications, especially in the field of hate speech when it comes to understanding why and how a model flags certain content. We believe our work could improve by exploring these approaches, as it would help researchers to better understand the decisions made by the models, and further create a human-in-the-loop approach, where they could provide feedback and refine these systems. Additionally, incorporating a gender bias analysis into our work could also be highly beneficial in this matter. For instance, examining how sports news \cite{sainzdebaranda-2013} and social media \cite{sainzdebaranda-2020} treat male and female athletes differently could provide deeper insights into the explainability of our models.

As discussed in Section~\ref{implementation_dl}, the high computational cost of deep learning experiments was a limiting factor in our study, restricting us to only a set of hyperparameters, due to the constraints of the hardware and software available. However, with the rise of cloud computing, particularly Cloud-as-a-Service (CaaS), there is a promising opportunity to overcome these limitations. In particular, future development could be studied to be used in cloud platforms like Google Colab\footnote{From \url{https://colab.research.google.com/}, last visited 24/08/24}, or for more powerful demands, enterprise-level cloud clusters.

In terms of Generative AI approaches, one possible direction of improvement is the integration of OpenAI's Moderation API endpoint\footnote{From \url{https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/moderation/overview}, last visited 24/08/24}, although "support for non-English languages is currently limited". Additionally, implementing GPT-4 turbo and GPT-4o with zero-shot and few-shot learning techniques could significantly enhance the detection capabilities of our system, and with the recent availability of fine-tuning for GPT-4o\footnote{From \url{https://openai.com/index/gpt-4o-fine-tuning/}, last visited 24/08/24} as of August 20th 2024, there is an exciting opportunity to tune these models more closely to our specific context, although careful consideration will be needed for pricing and data privacy.

In particular, one of our immediate goals is to apply our procedures and models to ongoing research being conducted by Professor Alba Adá for the Paris 2024 Summer Olympics. This research, which follows a similar approach as in \cite{lameiras-2021}, could be a valuable next step in testing our solution. Finally, the future goal of this work is to develop a user-friendly tool or interface that allows researchers to deploy and utilize our work easily. By creating this platform, our work could be applied across various tasks and fields, such as monitoring social media for harmful content against women athletes, analyzing sports news for gender bias and hate speech, and helping researchers and content moderators identify and address this content more effectively. Moreover, public institutions and organizations could use this work to create equality and social policies, public awareness campaigns and training resources for journalists, educators and influencers to help them recognize and address HS more effectively.
